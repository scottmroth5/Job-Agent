# Writing your knowledge doc

Your **knowledge doc** (Settings) is everything Job Hunt knows about you. Every score, cover letter, resume tweak, and meeting prep is written from it, and the AI is told to use only the facts in it. The more specific it is, the better they get. Think of it as the briefing you would give a recruiter who is going to represent you.

## How it is used

It has seven sections, numbered, because the AI's instructions refer to them by number:

| Section | What reads it |
|---|---|
| 1. Summary | Everything: the first impression of you |
| 2. Experience | Scoring, letters, and resume tweaks: the facts and numbers they may use |
| 3. Strengths | Scoring and letters: what to lead with |
| 4. Stories | Meeting prep and letters: what to tell |
| 5. Job Targets | **Scoring weighs this most.** Letters take their positioning from it |
| 6. Cover Letter Rules | Letters and resume tweaks follow every rule here |
| 7. Other | Anything that helps: education, certifications, languages |

Settings shows a check mark on each section it finds (a heading such as `## 5. Job Targets`). Your own titles are fine as long as the headings are numbered, such as `SECTION 5: TARGET ROLES`: the AI reads section 5 as your job targets and section 6 as your letter rules, whatever you call them. A missing **5. Job Targets** or **6. Cover Letter Rules** makes the biggest difference.

## Four ways to start

- **Start from the template:** in Settings, with the box empty, it puts in the seven headings with a line saying what goes under each. If your text is missing sections, the same button adds just those.
- **Build a draft:** share your resume and LinkedIn profile as PDFs (in Word: File, Save as, PDF; on LinkedIn: your profile, **More**, then **Save to PDF**) and anything else useful as text. Your AI writes all seven sections from them, marks gaps as **[add: …]**, and lists questions whose answers make the biggest difference. It costs a few cents; nothing is saved until you save it, and your files are not kept.
- **Fill from a document:** a PDF, Word .docx, or text file you already have; its text goes in the box for you to check and save. Moving from a Google Doc: in Google Docs choose **File, Download, Microsoft Word (.docx)**, then fill from that file.
- **Write it yourself** with the template below.

Then read it as the recruiter would, fix anything vague, and **Save**. It needs at least 500 characters; 2,000 to 6,000 is typical.

## Writing each section

**1. Summary.** Three or four sentences: your level and field, years, the industries you know, and the two or three things you are known for.

**2. Experience.** Role by role, newest first: title, company (or what the company does), years, and two to four results with numbers: team size, budget, revenue, growth, time or money saved, scale. The AI never adds numbers you did not write, so put the real ones here.

**3. Strengths.** What you do best, each with proof. "Hiring: built a structured interview loop and hired 80 engineers" beats "strong leader".

**4. Stories.** Two or three accomplishments you would tell in an interview: the situation, what you did, and the result. Meeting prep turns these into talking points.

**5. Job Targets.** Be concrete; scoring compares every posting with this section.
- Roles and level, industries, company size and stage.
- Places: remote, hybrid, or on site, and where; whether you would relocate.
- Pay, if you want it used. For part-time or fractional work, the rate and hours.
- **Not a fit:** roles, industries, or situations to avoid. This sharpens scoring as much as anything.
- **Positioning:** what to lead with for each kind of role you apply for, for example "for executive roles, lead with...; for hands-on roles, lead with...". Letters use the matching one.

**6. Cover Letter Rules.** How you want to sound: tone, length, words to avoid, and facts never to claim. Letters and resume tweaks follow every rule.

**7. Other.** Education, certifications, languages, volunteering, anything else useful.

Write plainly, in the first person. Facts beat adjectives.

## Template

```
## 1. Summary
[Your level and field] with [N] years in [industries]. Known for [two or three things].

## 2. Experience
**[Title], [Company or what it does] ([years])**
- [Result with a number]
- [Result with a number]

## 3. Strengths
- [Strength]: [proof]

## 4. Stories
1. **[Short title]:** [situation], [what you did], [result].

## 5. Job Targets
- **Roles:** [titles and level]
- **Industries:** [first choice; fine]
- **Company:** [size and stage]
- **Place:** [remote, hybrid, or on site, and where]
- **Pay:** [base or rate, if you want it used]
- **Not a fit:** [roles, industries, or situations to avoid]
- **Positioning:** for [kind of role], lead with [...]; for [kind of role], lead with [...].

## 6. Cover Letter Rules
- [Tone and length]
- Never use [words or phrases].
- Never claim [anything not in this doc].

## 7. Other
- [Education, certifications, languages]
```

## Example 1: an engineering leader

```
## 1. Summary
Engineering leader with 15 years in B2B software, the last 7 running teams of 30 to 120 engineers. Known for scaling platforms through fast growth and for hiring and on-call practices that last.

## 2. Experience
**VP of Engineering, a healthcare scheduling company (2021 to now)**
- Grew engineering from 35 to 110 across four product lines; attrition under 8%.
- Led the move from a monolith to services; deploys went from weekly to 40 a day.
**Director of Engineering, an analytics company (2016 to 2021)**
- Built the data platform behind a product line that reached $20M in annual revenue.
- Hired and coached five first-time managers.

## 3. Strengths
- Scaling teams: hired 80+ engineers with a structured interview loop.
- Reliability: cut major incidents 70% with on-call and review practices.
- Clear communication with boards and executives.

## 4. Stories
1. **Platform rescue:** inherited a platform with weekly outages; rebuilt on-call and testing; outages fell from 12 a quarter to 2 in nine months.
2. **Services move:** split the monolith one domain at a time over a year with no customer-facing outage.

## 5. Job Targets
- **Roles:** VP or SVP of Engineering; CTO at a smaller company.
- **Industries:** healthcare or fintech first; other B2B software is fine.
- **Company:** Series B to D, 50 to 300 engineers.
- **Place:** remote in the US, or hybrid near Anytown, ST.
- **Pay:** base of $250,000 or more.
- **Not a fit:** IT or infrastructure-only roles; agencies; roles without a product focus.
- **Positioning:** for VP and CTO roles, lead with scaling the organization and the services move; for director roles, lead with the platform rescue and hiring.

## 6. Cover Letter Rules
- Direct and specific, under 300 words. Open with the company's product and stage.
- Never use "passionate", "rockstar", or "I am confident".
- Never name past employers; describe what they do.

## 7. Other
- BS Computer Science. Speaker at two engineering leadership conferences.
```

## Example 2: a fractional finance leader

```
## 1. Summary
CFO with 20 years across software and consumer brands, now a fractional CFO for two or three companies at a time. Known for first operating models, board reporting, and fundraising.

## 2. Experience
**Fractional CFO, independent (2022 to now)**
- Built the first operating model and board pack for three seed to Series A startups.
- Led a $12M Series A process from model to close.
**CFO, a consumer products company (2015 to 2022)**
- Took revenue from $15M to $90M; ran finance, legal, and HR (22 people).

## 3. Strengths
- Fundraising: three rounds, $40M in total.
- Turning messy books into a monthly close in five days.

## 4. Stories
1. **Series A in eight weeks:** rebuilt the model, ran the data room, and closed at the top of the target range.

## 5. Job Targets
- **Roles:** fractional or interim CFO; part-time VP Finance.
- **Industries:** software and consumer brands.
- **Company:** seed to Series B.
- **Place:** fully remote.
- **Pay:** $200 to $300 an hour, 10 to 20 hours a week per client.
- **Not a fit:** full-time roles; bookkeeping-only work; companies without a product yet.
- **Positioning:** lead with fundraising and the first operating model.

## 6. Cover Letter Rules
- Short and practical: under 200 words, a bulleted list of what the first 90 days would deliver.
- Never claim a CPA license.

## 7. Other
- MBA. Board member of a local nonprofit.
```

## Example 3: changing fields

```
## 1. Summary
Senior product designer with 9 years in consumer apps, moving into product management. Known for research-led decisions and shipping with small teams.

## 2. Experience
**Lead Product Designer, a fitness app (2020 to now)**
- Ran the research and roadmap for onboarding; 30-day retention up from 22% to 31%.
- Acted as product owner for a squad of six for nine months while the PM role was open.

## 3. Strengths
- User research that changes decisions, with numbers to show it.
- Working day to day with engineers on scope and tradeoffs.

## 4. Stories
1. **Acting PM:** owned a squad's roadmap for nine months; shipped three features on time.

## 5. Job Targets
- **Roles:** Product Manager or Senior Product Manager; consumer apps.
- **Company:** 50 to 1,000 people.
- **Place:** remote, or on site in Anytown, ST.
- **Not a fit:** design-only roles; B2B infrastructure products.
- **Positioning:** lead with the acting PM work and the retention result; present design as a strength, not the job.

## 6. Cover Letter Rules
- Explain the move in one sentence, then show the product work.
- Never claim a Product Manager title I have not held.

## 7. Other
- Certificate in product management. Fluent in Spanish.
```

These examples are made up: write your own facts. The demo shows another full one, for a sample candidate, on its Settings page.

## Keeping it current

When something changes (a new role, a result, what you want next), edit and **Save**. New scores and letters use it right away. For jobs already scored or written, use **Re-score** or **Regenerate** on the job.

## Your resumes

**Settings > Resumes** keeps the resumes you actually send (PDF, Word .docx, or text). Give each a label and the job title words it is for, for example *VP, Head of, CTO* for an executive version. Each job's cover letter, resume tweaks, and meeting prep are written from the resume whose words match the job title, or else your default, and the job panel says which one. Resume tweaks then suggest changes to that resume. Scoring reads your knowledge doc only.

When you update a resume, use **Replace file**: the label and job titles stay. Letters already written are not changed; use **Regenerate** on a job to write them again from the new version.
