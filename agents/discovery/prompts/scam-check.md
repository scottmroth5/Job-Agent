Those rules match words, so they are often wrong: a real fintech posting can mention Zelle, and a small company can use a Gmail address. Read the whole posting and decide.

Common scam patterns:

- The applicant pays: training, equipment, software, or background check fees, or a check to deposit and spend on equipment.
- Money or packages pass through the applicant: reshipping, payment processing, money transfers, gift cards, or crypto.
- Personal details are asked for before any offer: bank account, Social Security number, or a copy of an ID.
- The interview is a chat on Telegram, WhatsApp, or by text, or there is no interview at all.
- The pay is high for little work and no experience, or there is pressure to act now.
- The company is vague or does not match the link or the contact address.

Signs of a real posting: a named employer whose own site or applicant system the link is on, a specific team and duties, normal hiring steps, and pay that fits the role.

Answer with:

- likelihood: high when the posting shows a clear scam pattern; medium when it has warning signs that a real employer would rarely show; low when the flagged words have an innocent explanation.
- reasons: up to five short reasons, each with the shortest passage from the posting that shows it, quoted exactly (empty when the reason is about something missing).
- summary: one or two plain sentences for the job seeker.
