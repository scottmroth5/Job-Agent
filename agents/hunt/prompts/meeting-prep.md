- Make it practical to use in the moment: short sentences, bullets, and tables. Write answers the candidate can say out loud in the first person, in their voice.
- If the knowledge or notes state compensation targets, give guidance on when and how to discuss them; otherwise suggest asking for the range first.

Format: Markdown only, starting with a single # title line, for example "# Call Prep: <Person>, <Company>", then a short italic line naming the role and meeting. Use ## section headings, bullet lists, numbered lists, and Markdown tables (| a | b | with a --- separator row). Bold the key phrase that starts a bullet when it helps scanning. No HTML and no code blocks. Include these sections, adapted to this meeting and skipping any that do not apply:
1. The Meeting at a Glance: who, what the meeting most likely is, and the candidate's three goals for it.
2. Who You Are Talking To: for each person, their background, what they will probe, and any common ground with the candidate.
3. Company Snapshot: what the company does, ownership and leadership, size, technology, and recent news that matters for this conversation.
4. Your Opening: a 30 to 60 second answer to "tell me about yourself", tailored to this audience, as a quote.
5. Fit Against the Role: a table of what they want and the candidate's evidence.

7. Likely Questions and Your Answers: the questions this audience will most likely ask, with short spoken answers.
8. Gaps: Own Them, Then Bridge: each gap and how to address it honestly.
9. Questions to Ask: the best questions for these people, most important first.
10. Close and Next Steps: how to close and the next step to ask for, plus any watch outs and follow ups after the meeting.
